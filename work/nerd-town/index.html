02Brief

An eBay-like shop. A crypto checkout. No product yet

The client came with an idea for a crypto marketplace — an eBay-like shop for collectibles, with bidding, a catalog and wallets — but no designed product yet. My job was to turn that list into an interface people could use: a cart that completes a purchase, a wallet that makes the currency feel normal, and an escrow that holds the money until the seller approves.

Without that interface the list would stay a list: a collector could find an item but not finish buying it, and Bitcoin Cash, not the collectibles, would be the first thing people saw.

How I read the brief

Not a Bitcoin Cash app that sells toys. A shop for collectibles, where Bitcoin Cash is the way to pay.

What the brief named

Goal

Unique items, bid on or bought in one shop

Let collectors browse one-of-a-kind items, place a bid or buy outright, and finish the deal in the same place it started.

Challenge

No email, and a stranger on the other side

No email keeps people anonymous but makes a lost password hard to get back, and every sale asks a buyer to send Bitcoin Cash to someone they have never met.

Need

Honest recovery and a wallet in the product

The product has to be honest about password recovery without an email, keep the Bitcoin Cash wallet inside the shop, and carry every purchase through to the end.

From the brief

The brief, summed up

An eBay-like shop for collectibles, with a catalog people can search and filter by franchise, character, year, and condition.

Sellers list an item and wait for a bid, or buyers purchase it directly.

Everything is paid in Bitcoin Cash, with wallets, and the money is held until the seller agrees to the sale.

No email, and buyers should see who they are dealing with before they pay.

Constraints

What the design holds to

  • Crypto in its place

    Bitcoin Cash shows on the prices and in the wallet, and stays quiet everywhere else.

  • Offer or buy

    Bidding was in the brief. The seller's List item dialog speaks of an offer or a direct purchase; the buyer's listing buys with Add to cart.

  • No email needed

    Signing up takes a username and a password, never an email address.

  • Held until approved

    A purchase waits in the buyer's wallet as Pending approval until the seller approves it, then reads Approved.

  • Money in the shop

    Deposit and withdraw are dialogs on the wallet page, with the address, the amount and the fee in view.

  • A phone of its own

    The phone has layouts of its own, not the desktop squeezed.

02 of / 04

Where Bitcoin Cash lives

Where does Bitcoin Cash live, if the product is a shop and not a wallet?

Judged on three tests

  1. 1Someone with no Bitcoin Cash yet can start with the items.
  2. 2A seller sees what a sale brought in without leaving the shop.
  3. 3A listing still looks like a listing.
  1. A

    Checkout only

    Connect, pay and forget: the shop keeps no balance.

    123Fails test 2: every payout is someone else's problem.

  2. B

    Wallet first

    The home screen is a balance, with the catalog behind it.

    123Fails test 1: the collectibles come second to a balance.

  3. Chosen

    A wallet beside the shop

    A wallet page of its own behind the header's icon, and a price in Bitcoin Cash on every listing.

    123Passes all three tests.

  • Why this one

    People come for an item or for a sale. The balance is the account behind both, so it stands beside the catalog, not in front of it.

  • Trade-off

    Two things to learn on a first visit: a shop and an account, not one screen.

  • Risk

    The wallet turns into a second product that the shop never mentions.

  • Reversibility

    The wallet can shrink back to a checkout later; growing a checkout into an account is the bigger change.

03 of / 04

When the money moves

When does the money actually move, if the other party is a stranger?

Judged on three tests

  1. 1A buyer has a reason to pay an unknown seller.
  2. 2A sale can go ahead before any parcel is tracked.
  3. 3The hold is a screen, not a sentence in the terms.
  1. A

    Instant transfer

    The money goes to the seller at checkout: the fastest way, with nothing held.

    123Fails tests 1 and 3: nothing is held, so nothing protects the buyer.

  2. B

    Hold until delivered

    The money waits until the parcel is marked as delivered.

    123Fails test 2: every sale would wait on parcel tracking.

  3. Chosen

    Hold for the seller's Approve

    Buy and the success screen come first; the seller then approves the sale, and a tracking number may follow.

    123Passes all three tests.

  • Why this one

    With no email and no bank between buyer and seller, the hold is how the shop earns a buyer's trust.

  • Trade-off

    A purchase is not over at the success screen: the buyer waits on the seller.

  • Risk

    A seller who never approves keeps the buyer's money waiting. The plan was for the hold to expire on its own.

  • Reversibility

    The hold can be made shorter later; taking it away would remove the buyer's only protection.

04 of / 04

How you sign in

How do you sign in to a crypto shop that does not want an email address?

  • Not an email login

    With an email login, the inbox becomes a second way in: anyone who gets into it can reset the password and take over the account.

  • Not a wallet login

    Signing in with a crypto wallet would ask a collector to have one before they could even join.

  • What a lost password costs

    A forgotten password can lock away more than an account here: the wallet sits behind it.

  • Adding email later

    An email field can be added later. Taking it away from people who signed up with one would be much harder.

08System

A shop that handles money needs one visual language

Most screens of Nerd Town ask for a decision: open an item or keep browsing, buy or close, approve a sale or wait. Colour, type and controls are decided once, so each choice looks the same wherever it comes up, in the catalog, in a dialog or on a wallet row.

The style guide sets a green primary with a dark ink and a pale mint, and Poppins for headings and body. On the screens the green is the step forward and money coming in, the mint is the choice beside it, and a grey-green sage carries the details. A deep red marks money going out, and orange is kept for the Bitcoin Cash mark beside an amount.

10Reflection

What I’d keep, change and validate next

  • What I’d keep

    The hold behind every step

    After Buy, the success screen’s promised update, the wallet’s statuses and the parcel step all rest on it.

  • What I’d change

    One rule for both sides

    The Sell Stuff door says to wait for the highest bid, yet no buyer can bid. I’d add a bid next to Add to cart.

  • What I’d validate next

    Trust, with no data

    Is a seller’s card with no reviews enough to pay a stranger? Does Pending approval tell a buyer enough?
