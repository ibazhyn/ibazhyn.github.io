02Brief

The product idea existed. The experience didn't

The client brought the concept and the capabilities it wanted. There was no existing product to redesign, so the whole experience, from logging an hour to billing it, was still to be designed.

Without that experience the capabilities would stay a pile of separate tools: hours could not be billed, projects could not show their cost, and a manager could see neither.

How I read the brief

Not a screen for each capability. One workspace where logging, costing and billing are the same hours, seen from three seats.

What the brief named

Goal

A true picture of productivity and cost

Hours that can be costed, not only counted: where the time went, what that time cost and how much of it can be billed.

Challenge

Inefficiencies were invisible

When cost sits in a different tool from the hours, nobody sees which project or task takes more time and money than it should.

Need

An interface people would open every day

Each team sets up tracking per client or per project, and the reports come from the same hours the team logged.

From the brief

The client listed capabilities. Nothing yet tied them together. Three scenes: the client brief, the seats that work in the product, and the UX flows that followed.

A time tracking product

The client listed capabilities. Nothing yet tied them together.

The brief, summed up

A time tracking product where people can start a timer, add hours by hand, or fill in a timesheet for the whole week.

Every hour belongs to a project and a client. Tags and templates too, so nobody sets the same things up twice.

Then the money side: expenses, plus reports and invoices made from those same hours. Someone has to approve the hours before they are billed. Some teams track per client, others per project.

iOS and Android as well — people log time on the go.

Constraints

What the design holds to

  • One task

    However the hours go in, every entry has the same project, client, task and billable fields.

  • Any basis

    The tracking basis is one setting in Business Details, not a different product.

  • A native phone

    The phone is a native surface, not the desktop squeezed.

  • Settings stay settings

    Templates, tags and categories exist, but they never become the product.

  • Billable, end to end

    Billable is one switch on the row, and reports and invoices use that same switch.

  • A dialog, not a toast

    Trial expiry is a dialog: while days are left it can be closed, but once the trial has ended it offers only Log Out or Activate.

02 of / 04

One decision

How should someone log time, when the same hours later become a timesheet, an approval and a line on an invoice?

Judged on three tests

  1. 1A row still says how it was entered.
  2. 2Starting a timer is never slower than opening a sheet.
  3. 3A manager sees the same rows the logger wrote.
  1. A

    One smart field

    It guesses tracker, manual entry or timesheet from what you type.

    123Fails test 1: a guess hides how the row was entered.

  2. B

    Timesheet only

    The week is the unit, and a timer is an extra.

    123Fails test 2: there is no timer to start when the work starts.

  3. Chosen

    Three methods as tabs

    Tracker, manual entry and timesheet, each first-class, sharing project, client and billable.

    123Passes all three tests.

  • Why this one

    People log hours while the work happens, after it, or as a sheet at the end of the week. Each moment has its own tab; one project model keeps them one task.

  • Trade-off

    More chrome in Time: a first-time logger picks a method before a project.

  • Risk

    Someone lives in one method and never finds the other two.

  • Reversibility

    A fourth method is one more tab. Merging two methods later means moving every row already logged.

03 of / 04

How time is logged

The chosen answer, as the delivered design shows it.

  • Three tabs of Time

    Tracker, Manual and Timesheet are tabs of one area, not three items in the nav.

  • Each method can be off

    A business turns the tracker, manual entry and the timesheet on or off in its setup.

  • Two methods on the phone

    The phone keeps tracking and manual entry; it has no timesheet.

  • Three phone tab bars

    Three different tab bars were drawn for the phone app.

04 of / 04

From hours to invoice

Cost does not wait for a report: it shows on the project while the hours are logged, and the invoice is made from those hours.

  • Cost on the project

    A project's overview shows its cost next to its hours and billable share.

  • Approval has its own area

    Timesheet approval and manager approval sit together in Approvals.

  • Invoices from logged hours

    An invoice takes logged hours, all or approved only, with the timelog attached.

The product has three jobs. Each has its own place in the main navigation: Time, Reports and Accounting.

1

Track time

Hours logged against a project and a client

The timer runs against a project, a client, a task and a subtask, and one switch marks the hours billable. Below it, each logged entry shows its duration, whether it is billable and its status, Logged or Submitted. Above the entries sit a date range and a search, with filters for clients, projects, users and tags.

2

Report and visualize

The same hours as totals, a daily chart and groups

Analysis takes a date range, filtered by client, project and user, and shows total, billable and non-billable hours, a bar for each day, and the hours grouped by client, project, task and subtask. Two dashboards follow: Business, with cost, profit and invoiced revenue, and Team, with each member's hours against capacity.

3

Invoice and get paid

Logged hours priced into an invoice

Step one of an invoice lists each project's hours, approved and unapproved, billable and non-billable, beside its expenses. Step two gives each project one line, its duration times its charge rate, then adds tax and a discount. Receivables show the paid and overdue totals, and each invoice is marked Created, Sent, Paid or Overdue.

07States

Beyond the happy path

  1. 1

    Idle

    Nothing is being tracked: a Start tracking button sits where the running timer would be.

    On the phone, Start is inactive until the form is filled in.

  2. 2

    Trial ending

    Before the trial runs out, a dialog says how many days are left and offers Continue or Activate.

    Once it has expired, the × is gone: only Log Out or Activate.

  3. 3

    Near limit

    When someone enters time for another user, an orange banner warns that the project is close to its maximum hours.

    A dialog lists each project’s maximum hours per month.

  4. 4

    Limit exceeded

    Once the maximum is passed, the banner turns red and shows the hours logged against it.

    The row stays editable, and Submit timesheet stays in place.

08System

A workspace this wide needs one visual language

Productively has ten areas on the web, and phone apps for logging time, expenses and trips. It only holds together if colour, type and controls are decided once. That is what makes tracking, approvals, reports and invoices feel like one product, not four tools that happen to share a logo.

The style guide sets a blue primary, a green secondary and Poppins for headings and body. The blue marks navigation, the timer and the current tab, and the screens use it in shades, from the pale fill of a filter chip to the deep blue of the current area. The green marks completed work and positive cost, and each status is a strong colour on a pale fill of the same hue.

10Reflection

What I’d keep, change and validate next

  • What I’d keep

    Hours carry into the invoice

    A new invoice starts from the logged hours, can take approved hours only, and attaches the timelog.

  • What I’d change

    One name for one area

    One nav item reads Accounting on the base screens and Invoices on others. I’d pick one, as one area needs one label.

  • What I’d validate next

    Two questions with no data

    Do people find the Manual and Timesheet tabs? Does approved-only billing fit how teams bill?
