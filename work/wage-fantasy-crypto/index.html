02Brief

Fantasy sports plus crypto: an idea without a product yet

The client wanted fantasy sports and cryptocurrency in one product, where good strategy could win real money. What did not exist yet was the experience: how to join a league, read its rules and build a team. My job was to design it: league terms anyone can read before joining, a team a newcomer can set up, and a wallet where the winnings arrive.

What the brief was really asking for

Not “fantasy football with a wallet.” A league where the coins themselves are the players.

What the brief named

Goal

A game played on real coin prices

Real crypto performance decides the week, and the prize is paid out inside the same product.

Challenge

Seeing why a team won

A player had to see how a change in a coin’s price turns into a win. That link was the real gap in the idea.

Need

Clear rules and a readable team

The league’s rules on view before anyone pays, and a roster that shows what each position holds and how much it counts.

From the brief

Fantasy sports whose roster is crypto

The brief, summed up

Fantasy sports where the sport is crypto: instead of players, people pick coins, and the market decides who wins.

They join a league, or create one with their own rules, and build a team for the week.

It has to be a week that resolves fairly, and the winners get paid into a wallet.

It should work for newcomers and for fantasy players, on a desktop and on a phone.

Constraints

What the design holds to

  • Weights in the open

    Every position shows its weight, and the nine weights add up to 100%.

  • Terms up front

    Before a join, the league’s page shows the prize fund and its split, the join fee, the number of players, access, start time and game interval; the join dialog adds the early withdrawal fee.

  • One wallet

    Deposits, join fees, winnings and withdrawals all go through one wallet inside the hub.

  • Laid out for the phone

    On the phone, the four main areas sit in a menu, and the wallet shows its assets and its transactions as two tabs.

04Goals

What a player had to be able to do

These are the brief’s service goals and key challenges as design jobs, not measured outcomes: what a player had to be able to do, from finding a league to keeping up with friends. Each card shows the part of the delivered screen that does it.

  1. 1

    A league that fits

    Browse lists the leagues in one table with their start, game interval, join fee, teams, prize split and access, and a row offers Open, or Request To Join for a private league.

  2. 2

    Terms set by its creator

    A player can start a league of their own, setting its join fee, early withdrawal fee and prize split on the first step, beside its access, size, start and interval.

  3. 3

    Missing funds in red

    Setting up a team, a newcomer sees at once which coin they cannot cover: it shows in red, with Deposit The Missing Funds under its position.

  4. 4

    A result every week

    Each week ends in a result a player can check: the winner and the loser side by side, and each position’s coin with its change that week.

  5. 5

    A name on each prize

    In the playoffs, the prize bar puts a player’s name on each place, so everyone in the league sees who holds which share of the prize.

  6. 6

    Friends and their leagues

    Friends have a list of their own, with what each is playing and their wins and losses, and a friend’s page sorts their leagues into wins, losses, playing and their own.

05Solution

Making rules, team creation and the wallet feel like one product

I designed the product from league discovery to team creation, weekly standings and the wallet. It had to feel like a game, not a trading screen: each position holds a coin with a public weight, the league shows its terms before anyone joins, and every join, team change and payout has a clear next step, on desktop and on the phone.

02 of / 04

Who plays on a team

What should a team be made of, when the game runs on coin prices?

Judged on three tests

  1. 1The coins a player picks shape the result.
  2. 2A newcomer can read a team without trading.
  3. 3There is a league to join and weeks to win.
  1. A

    Athletes with a coin badge

    Fantasy football as it is, with crypto only on the surface.

    123Fails test 1: the athletes decide the result, not the coins.

  2. B

    A charting terminal

    The market itself, with no league around it.

    123Fails tests 2 and 3: it asks for trading and has no league.

  3. Chosen

    Coins in football positions

    A team keeps the positions of football, with a coin in each.

    123Passes all three tests.

  • Why this one

    It is the one answer that is a game and a market at once: fans know the positions, and the coins bring the market in.

  • Trade-off

    Football fans have to learn coin tickers as players.

  • Risk

    Someone who came for a game may see only a market and leave.

  • Reversibility

    Athletes could be added later as a second kind of team. Taking the coins out would make it a different product.

03 of / 04

What a position is worth

With a coin in every position, how should a player know what each position is worth?

Judged on three tests

  1. 1It matters which position holds which coin.
  2. 2A player reads the rule where the team is set.
  3. 3A newcomer does not have to calculate anything.
  1. A

    Raw price change

    Every coin counts the same, whatever position it holds.

    123Fails test 1: the position makes no difference.

  2. B

    A formula in a FAQ

    The rule is explained only after someone has joined.

    123Fails test 2: the rule is out of sight when the team is set.

  3. Chosen

    Public weights

    Each position carries a weight, set by the league’s creator and printed on the team.

    123Passes all three tests.

  • Why this one

    With its weights on it, a team reads like a football line-up: the positions that count most are plain to see.

  • Trade-off

    More to read before the first join: nine weights as well as the league’s terms.

  • Risk

    A newcomer may read 25% as a chance to win, not as a share of the fee.

  • Reversibility

    The weights could later be fixed for every league. Hiding them again, once leagues had run on them, would change the game.

The whole join fee 100%

Three features run through the hub: friends, the multipliers and the weekly updates. Each has its own place: the Friends area, a week’s match, and a league’s page with Joined Leagues.

1

Friends across the hub

In the header, on a player’s page and in Browse Leagues

Friends is one of the four areas in the header, next to Browse Leagues, Joined Leagues and Wallet, and its list of friends can be searched by name. A player’s page has an Add Friend button, and Browse Leagues has a Friend’s tab beside All, Upcoming and Featured.

2

Multipliers in a match

The week’s match, position by position, in Detailed Mode

A week’s match has a Detailed Mode switch. Turned on, it adds a Multiplier and a Final Value to every position, and a total under each player’s table.

3

The season, week by week

A mark a week, and where each joined league stands

A league’s page gives every player a W, an L or a D for each week, with a clock on the week still to come, and arrows to step through the weeks. Joined Leagues lists every league a player is in, each with its score and its result: Playing, Winner or Loser.

08System

Every colour has one job

Almost every screen of the hub is full of figures: fees, prize shares, a week’s result, a wallet’s amounts. A player has to tell them apart at a glance, in a table on Browse Leagues as much as in a match, so each colour keeps one meaning on every screen.

Teal is what a player acts on and navy is what they read, with paler blues and a grey a step below. Gold, a blue-grey and orange mark first, second and third place. Green and red mark a win and a loss, and in the wallet the money coming in and going out; a draw has a yellow of its own.

10Reflection

What I’d keep, change and validate next

  • What I’d keep

    Everything set but the coins

    Positions and weights come with the league, so the coins are the only choice a team leaves to its player.

  • What I’d change

    Two numbers, two names

    A team’s weights and a match’s details both say Multiplier, for two different numbers. I’d give each its own name.

  • What I’d validate next

    Two questions, no data

    Do players turn on Detailed Mode at all? Do people joining a league look for the mid-game rule its page leaves out?
